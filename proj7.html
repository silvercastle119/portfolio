AI Safety Navigating App

AI-based Personalized Map App for Safer Route Guidance, UX/UI Design, and Test-driven Iteration

Date

Mar 2025 – Jan 2026

Role

Product Manager (PM), UX/UI Designer

Tools

Figma, Python, Oracle SQL (SQL Developer), Tmap API, Public Data Portal, Notion, GitHub

Category

AI Product, Accessibility, UX/UI, User Research, Mapping, Testing & Iteration

Overview

As part of KFAS, I led product planning for an AI-based safety navigation app designed to help pedestrians find safer, easier-to-follow routes through personalized guidance. As PM and UX/UI Designer, I owned the full product cycle — from problem definition and user research to feature planning, developer communication, testing, and iterative improvement. I connected API-sourced infrastructure data, usability-driven UX design, and a test-based feedback loop into one coherent product flow so safety signals could translate into real, confident route decisions.

Challenge

Existing pedestrian navigation services optimize primarily for shortest distance, failing to account for diverse user needs — including age, accessibility, and varying levels of map literacy. In particular, route complexity and difficulty were not factored in, meaning "short but confusing and unsafe routes" were routinely recommended. The core gap was not a lack of data, but the absence of a product layer that could translate safety and accessibility signals into a route experience users could actually understand and trust.

Strategy

Difficulty-first route design

I redefined "safe route" beyond hazard avoidance to mean a path that is easy to follow and low in cognitive load for any user. Feature design and screen flow were structured around reducing route difficulty — measured through risk segment count, complexity score, and detour rate — rather than just displaying safety information.

Infrastructure data layer + test-driven iteration

I built the routing foundation by collecting safety-relevant infrastructure signals — streetlights, convenience stores, police stations, foot traffic, benches, and major buildings — via Tmap API and the Public Data Portal. Product assumptions were then validated and refined through repeated alpha/beta testing cycles, with user feedback directly shaping each iteration.

Process

01

Research

Conducted user interviews with 50 participants across ages 20–60 to identify pain points in pedestrian navigation — particularly around route comprehension, safety perception, and the difficulty of following existing map guidance. Benchmarked existing services (including Naver Maps) to map where users with different characteristics experienced confusion or drop-off.

02

Insight

Diagnosed the core gap as: shortest-path optimization fails to reflect route difficulty and individual usability, creating a disconnect between "technically correct" navigation and what users can actually follow safely. Defined the key unlock as redesigning route recommendation logic — and the UX around it — to reduce difficulty as a first-class metric, not an afterthought.

03

Planning & Design

Planned core app features (personalized routing, difficulty/safety signals, guidance clarity) and designed alpha/beta test scenarios and survey instruments to validate each assumption. Built the UX/UI framework in Figma, researching accessibility standards from comparable services and applying principles around color contrast, font sizing, and object spacing to maximize clarity for all users.

04

Build, Test, Iterate

Collected and structured infrastructure data via Tmap API and the Public Data Portal, then worked closely with developers to translate product requirements into implementable specs. Ran alpha (10 users) and beta (10 users) test rounds to gather structured feedback, driving iterative fixes; additionally used Oracle SQL-based social data analysis to extract user sentiment on existing map apps and feed findings into prioritization.

Execution

User research & problem framing

I grounded the product in real user context by conducting 50 interviews and synthesizing findings into a clear problem definition: route difficulty, not just safety, was the primary barrier. I also ran Oracle SQL-based social listening on existing map app reviews to surface recurring complaints and validate the research direction with quantitative signal.

Feature planning & UX/UI design

I planned the full feature set for the app and designed the UX/UI framework directly in Figma, referencing third-party accessibility design systems to apply contrast, scale, and spacing standards. Each screen was built around reducing decision fatigue — making safety-relevant information immediately readable without adding cognitive load to the route experience.

Data collection & developer collaboration

I collected infrastructure data (streetlights, convenience stores, police stations, foot traffic, benches, major buildings) via Tmap API and Korea's Public Data Portal and structured it for routing use. As PM, I communicated requirements to the development team and ensured product intent translated accurately into the built experience.

Alpha/Beta testing & iteration

I designed and operated both alpha (10 users) and beta (10 users) test rounds, collecting structured feedback through surveys and follow-up sessions. Each round produced a clear list of usability issues and feature gaps, which I prioritized and fed back into design and development — reducing route difficulty by 60% and lifting user satisfaction by 40% compared to existing navigation services.
